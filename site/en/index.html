Did the polling place changes for the runoff favor either candidate?

Brazil's presidential runoff between Lula and Flávio Bolsonaro is on October 25. I matched the official list of polling sections that changed location for the runoff with each section's first-round votes, and tested whether the changes hit one candidate's sections more than chance would explain.

From 4,352 down to 441

The number of moved sections dropped over five days. The electoral court's (TSE) system was counting sections that stayed at the same address as "changed", and between Oct 19 and Oct 20 most of the changes were undone (section further down).

The test: are the schools that moved different from their neighbors?

If someone deliberately picked the schools where one candidate is strongest, the moved schools would lean more toward that candidate than the other schools in the same zone. The test compares what happened with 5,000 random draws. (In Brazil, most polling places are schools.)

Lula's share (Lula vs. Flávio) in moved schools: actual × 5,000 draws

Each bar is how many draws landed on that share. The gray band holds 95% of the draws.

  1. In each of the electoral zones with at least one change, I counted how many schools actually moved.
  2. In each zone, I drew the same number of schools at random and computed Lula's share of the votes cast for Lula or Flávio in those schools.
  3. I repeated the draw 5,000 times. The result is what a change with no political criterion at all would produce.

Method and limitations

Why compare within the zone. Polling place changes happen more where there are rural schools, power outages and heavy rain at this time of year, and those regions voted more for Lula. Comparing the moved sections with the whole country shows a difference that comes from the place, not from the choice. Within the same electoral zone, the city, the urban or rural profile and the judge who decides are the same on both sides.

Why the unit is the school. The decision to move is made per polling place: when a school closes, all of its sections move together. Counting section by section would give too much weight to large schools.

The 95% band. It is the range that holds 95% of the draws, and describes what chance produces. It is not a confidence interval: it does not estimate a true value, it only shows the expected variation if schools had been picked with no criterion. The p value is the share of draws that landed as far from the average as the actual value, or farther. Below 0.05, the result would be considered unusual.

What the test does not answer. Correlation does not prove intent, and the absence of correlation does not prove the change didn't hurt anyone. The real impact can be measured after Oct 25, by comparing runoff abstention in the moved sections with their neighbors.

Why the dropped changes are drawn within each state. No zone had only part of its changes undone: either every school in the zone went back to its old place, or none did. There is nothing to draw within a zone, so the draw is made among schools in the same state, where the regional court decides.

  • The TSE dashboard only shows the current version of the list. The Oct 19 one is in the repository because it was downloaded that day, and the Oct 16 and Oct 18 versions exist only as figures given to the press.
  • The TSE overwrites the polling place registry at every update. The classification of the Oct 19 list (made against the Oct 18 registry) was saved, because that registry version cannot be downloaded again.
  • For the changes that were dropped, the data cannot tell whether they were undone or the Oct 18 registry was wrong. In the current registry, those sections vote at their first-round place.
  • The split of off-list changes into real, same street with a new number, doubtful and spelling differences was done by hand and is in data/revisao/ for anyone to check.
